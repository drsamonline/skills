# Metadata, rights and pricing

The book is published; this file is about what the storefront shows and what
you get paid. Nothing here is printed inside the book.

## Title block

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Title | The main title. Do not put the subtitle here. |
| Subtitle | Appears after the title on the detail page. Keep under ~200 characters. |
| Series | Only if the books genuinely share a series name. KDP links volumes by exact string match. |
| Author | Legal or pen name. Must match the account or be a declared pen name. |
| Imprint | Optional publisher name. Leave blank if self-publishing without one. |
| Contributors | Editors, translators, illustrators — each with a role. |
| Language | Drives the storefront and the ISBN metadata. |

## The blurb

KDP allows up to 4,000 characters. It is the single highest-leverage marketing
asset on the page.

Structure that works for non-fiction:

1. **One line that names the problem or the promise.** Not a tagline — a
   sentence a reader would recognise as their own situation.
2. **What the book is, concretely.** Scope, count, structure. Numbers help.
3. **What makes it different.** The thing a competing book does not do.
4. **Who it is for.** Say it plainly.
5. **Close with credibility or a call to action.**

Keep it plain text unless you need bold — KDP supports limited HTML
(`<b>`, `<i>`, `<br>`, `<ul>`) in the blurb field, but formatting does not
survive everywhere it is syndicated. Plain text is safer.

Avoid: "bestselling", "award-winning", competitor names, price claims, review
quotes you cannot attribute, and any medical or legal promise. KDP rejects
blurbs that make health claims.

## Categories

Pick up to **three**. Use BISAC paths rather than free-text, e.g.

- `HEA046000` Health & Fitness / Homeopathy
- `MED071000` Medical / Pharmacology
- `REF000000` Reference

Categories are competitive positioning, not description. Choose the ones where
the book can actually rank rather than the biggest ones.

## Keywords

Seven slots. They are search terms, not a description.

- Use phrases a reader would type, not single words.
- Do not repeat words already in the title or subtitle — KDP indexes those
  already, so a keyword slot spent repeating them is wasted.
- Do not use competitor author names or trademarked terms.
- No "free", "bestseller", "new", or price words.

## ISBN

| Format | ISBN |
|---|---|
| eBook | none — Amazon assigns an ASIN |
| Paperback | KDP gives a **free** ISBN, or you supply your own |
| Hardcover | same as paperback |

The free KDP ISBN is registered to KDP as publisher and **cannot be used with
another printer**. If you may print elsewhere, buy your own.

An ISBN is required for print and is locked to the trim, binding and ink type at
publication. Changing any of those later means a new edition and a new ISBN.

Never print the ISBN inside the book unless you want it there — KDP adds a
barcode to the back cover from the ISBN you registered.

## Pricing and royalty

| Format | Royalty |
|---|---|
| eBook | 35%, or 70% inside a price band set by marketplace |
| Paperback | 60% of list price **minus printing cost** (Amazon), 40% for expanded distribution |
| Hardcover | 60% of list price minus printing cost |

Printing cost scales with page count and ink type — a 750-page black-and-white
book costs substantially more to print than a 200-page one, and premium colour
costs more again. **Use KDP's own printing cost calculator for the actual
figure**; do not estimate it, because it changes by marketplace and has changed
over time.

Practical consequences of the royalty structure:

- There is a **minimum list price** below which KDP will not let you publish,
  because it would not cover the print cost. Long books have a high floor.
- Expanded distribution (40%) pays less but reaches bookshops and libraries.
- Lowering the price to "be competitive" on a 750-page book can put you under
  the print cost. Check the floor before setting a price.

## Rights and territories

- Choose worldwide rights unless you have sold territorial rights to a
  publisher.
- Public-domain source material does **not** make your compilation public
  domain — the selection, arrangement and description are your copyright. Say so
  in the copyright page; it prevents a reviewer assuming the whole book is free
  to copy.

## Pre-publication checklist for the metadata

- [ ] Title and subtitle do not duplicate each other's keywords
- [ ] Blurb is under 4,000 characters and contains no health or legal claims
- [ ] Exactly three categories, each a real BISAC path
- [ ] Seven keywords, no repeats of title words, no competitor names
- [ ] ISBN decision made (free KDP vs own) and recorded
- [ ] List price is above the print-cost floor for this page count and ink
- [ ] Territories match the rights you actually hold
- [ ] Author name matches the account or is a declared pen name
