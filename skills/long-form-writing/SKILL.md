# Long-form writing (books and content)

Two scales of the same craft. **Content** is a brief to a channel-ready draft in one pass. **A book** is phases, not steps: foundation, manuscript with an adversarial audit per chapter, design, assembly, build. Both refuse to invent a fact, a quote, a statistic or a source, and both treat voice as a constraint rather than a flourish.

## When to use

- A blog post, newsletter, landing-page copy or social copy is wanted from a brief.
- A book or series must be drafted, translated, restyled, or packaged into DOCX/PDF/HTML editions.
- Existing copy must be revised for a different channel or language.

## Method

1. Extract the brief before writing: goal (what the reader should think or do), audience, channel, key message, and the must-include facts or offers. If one is missing, ask rather than guess.
2. For content, pick the channel playbook and match its shape and length; never let the format override the message.
3. Write clear, warm, concrete prose: active voice, short sentences, second person, no clichés or buzzwords. If the brief names a tone or house voice, match it exactly.
4. Run the human-voice check before delivering — plain language, varied rhythm, banned AI vocabulary.
5. For another language, translate the finished draft for meaning and tone first, never word for word; localise calls to action.
6. Never fabricate a statistic, quote, source or name. Search for the fact, attribute it plainly in the copy.
7. For a book, run the five phases in order and finish every task inside a phase before moving on: foundation (title, blurb, metadata, author bio, chapter plan), manuscript (draft, then adversarial fact audit per chapter, then a deduplication pass against the registry), design (per chapter, from the design system), assembly (front and back matter), build (fixed-page A4 HTML master, DOCX, PDF, and the combined multi-language HTML with a language switcher).
8. Keep book metadata (title, blurb, categories, keywords) in its own file — never merged into the manuscript.
9. Verify the build output page by page; a chapter that renders is not a book that reads.
10. You draft; you do not publish, send or schedule unless explicitly asked.

## Gotchas

- A draft with no stated goal reads well and does nothing.
- Adversarial fact audit is per chapter and before design — auditing after layout doubles the rework.
- Two chapters making the same point is a registry failure, not an editing problem.
- Never invent a fact to keep a sentence's rhythm.
- Translation that follows the source word order reads as translation.
