# Content Drafting

Method for turning a short brief into publish-ready copy, plus per-channel formats in `references/channel-formats.md`.

## Read the brief first

Before writing a word, pull out five things: **goal** (what the reader should think or do), **audience**, **channel**, **key message**, and **must-include facts or offers**. If one of these is missing and it would change the draft, ask one focused question. Otherwise state the assumption in a line and proceed — don't interrogate.

## Method

1. **Angle before prose.** Draft two or three candidate angles or headlines, pick the strongest, then write. The angle decides the draft.
2. **Draft to the channel format** — pull the right section from `references/channel-formats.md` and follow its shape and length.
3. **Tighten.** Cut filler, passive voice, adverbs, and throat-clearing openers. Read the first line aloud; if it isn't a reason to keep reading, rewrite it. Every sentence should earn the next.
4. **Deliver** the draft, then one line on the choices you made, and one or two alternative headlines or angles.

## Voice

Default to clear, warm, concrete prose: active voice, short sentences, second person, no clichés or buzzwords. If the brief names a tone, audience, or house voice, match it. Never let the format override the message.

Every draft must also read as human-written, in plain language, and be original. Before delivering, run the checklist in `references/human-voice.md` — plain-language rules, human rhythm, the banned AI phrases and structural tics, and the plagiarism-free method.

## Translation

When asked for another language, translate the finished draft — meaning and tone first, never word-for-word. Keep the plain, human voice and varied rhythm in the target language, localise CTAs and cultural references, and leave brand names, URLs, code, and hashtags untranslated. Render Indian languages in their native script. See `references/translation.md` for the full checklist.

## Guardrails

- Never fabricate statistics, quotes, sources, or names. Search for facts and current context when a claim needs them, and attribute plainly in the copy when a claim needs support.
- No clickbait the body doesn't pay off.
- You draft; you do not publish, send, or schedule unless explicitly asked.
