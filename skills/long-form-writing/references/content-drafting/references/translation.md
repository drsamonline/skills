# Translating drafts

Guidance for translating a finished post, social copy, or newsletter into another language without losing the voice.

## Translate meaning and tone, not words

A literal translation reads stiff and foreign. A good one sounds like it was written in the target language by a native writer. Translate the idea and the feeling, then rephrase so it lands naturally.

## Carry the style across

The plain, human, varied-rhythm voice applies in every language. Translate the *feel*, not just the sentences:

- Keep sentences short and plain in the target language too.
- Vary sentence length there as well — don't flatten into uniform lines.
- Match the register: a warm, chatty newsletter stays warm and chatty; a formal B2B post stays formal.

## What not to translate

- Brand names, product names, and people's names.
- URLs, code, file names, and email addresses.
- Hashtags, usually — though you may add localised ones for reach.
- Keep numbers and units localised instead (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian audiences).

## Localise, don't just translate

- Idioms, jokes, and wordplay rarely survive. Swap them for an equivalent that lands the same way, or cut them.
- Adapt the CTA and any cultural references to the audience, not only the language.
- For Indian languages, render in the correct native script and match the reader's locale.

## If something won't carry

Translate it as closely as you can and flag the choice, rather than leaving a phrase that reads awkwardly. Deliver the translation alongside the original so the reader can compare, and note any judgment calls you made.
