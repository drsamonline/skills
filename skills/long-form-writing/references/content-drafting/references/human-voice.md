# Human voice, plain language, and clean copy

Rules and checklists for making drafts read like a person wrote them: easy to read, original, free of AI tells.

## Plain language

- Short words, short sentences, one idea per sentence. Target roughly an eighth-grade reading level.
- Prefer the concrete to the abstract: "cut the report to two pages", not "optimise reporting efficiency".
- Active voice and second person ("you") by default.
- Explain a necessary term the first time it appears, or replace it. Never stack jargon.
- Cut every word that isn't working: "in order to" → "to", "at this point in time" → "now", "utilise" → "use".

## Human rhythm

- Vary sentence length on purpose. A three-word sentence after a long one lands hard.
- Use contractions — "you're", "it's", "don't". They read as speech.
- Start the occasional sentence with "And" or "But". Real writing does.
- Write to one person, not a crowd. "You" beats "users" or "customers".
- Use specific, lived detail over general claims. One real example beats three vague ones.

## Banned AI phrases and tics

Avoid these — they are the fingerprints of machine-written copy:

- **Filler and hedges:** "it's worth noting", "it's important to note", "it's no secret that", "needless to say", "at the end of the day", "when it comes to", "in essence", "ultimately", "that being said".
- **Hype words:** "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "robust", "leverage", "game-changer", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "revolutionise", "empower", "unleash", "supercharge", "next-level".
- **Openers and closers:** "in today's fast-paced world", "in this day and age", "look no further", "we've got you covered", "in conclusion", "to sum up", "let's dive in", "delve into", "embark on a journey", "without further ado".
- **Transition crutches:** "moreover", "furthermore", "additionally", "that said", used mechanically at the start of sentences.
- **Metaphor clichés:** "tapestry", "testament to", "navigate the landscape", "the realm of", "double-edged sword", "tip of the iceberg", "uncharted waters", "journey".
- **Corporate speak:** "synergy", "circle back", "move the needle", "low-hanging fruit", "touch base", "at scale", "best-in-class".

## Structural AI tells

- The "not only X, but Y" and "it's not just X, it's Y" construction, used more than once.
- Stacked triads ("fast, reliable, and scalable") where one concrete claim would do.
- Every paragraph the same length and shape. Break the pattern.
- A rhetorical question followed by its own answer, as a tic.
- Em-dash overuse — fine once or twice, a tell when every other sentence has one.

## Plagiarism-free by construction

- Never copy a sentence from a source. Read it, close it, write the idea in your own words.
- Keep any direct quote short and always name the source.
- Facts are free to reuse; the wording is not. Rephrase, restructure, and re-order.
- When in doubt, rewrite from scratch rather than paraphrasing word-by-word.

## On AI detectors

Detectors are unreliable and flag human writing too. Don't write to beat a tool — write so the copy sounds like a real person: specific, varied, plain, and free of the tics above. Never promise a "passes AI detection" score; promise writing that reads as human.
